I hear your frustration—wireless printer setup can be a real headache! Let’s troubleshoot step by step to get you printing today.

First, double-check the printer’s wireless setup method. Many printers use a "WPS" (Wi-Fi Protected Setup) button, a PIN code, or a mobile app (like HP Smart, Canon PRINT, or Epson Connect). If you’re using WPS, press the WPS button on your router and then the WPS button on the printer within 2 minutes. If using a PIN, note the 8-digit code displayed on the printer’s screen and enter it on your router’s admin page or via the setup app.

Next, confirm the printer and your device are on the same network—especially if you have a dual-band router (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz). Most older printers only support 2.4 GHz, not 5 GHz. Check your router settings to ensure the 2.4 GHz network is active and visible. If your printer is connected to 5 GHz, it won’t see the 2.4 GHz network, and vice versa.

Try resetting the printer’s network settings. Look for a "Network" or "Wireless Setup" option in the printer’s menu, then choose "Reset Network Settings" or "Remove Network." Then restart the setup process.

Also, check for firmware updates on the printer. Outdated firmware can cause connection issues. Visit the manufacturer’s website, enter your printer model, and download any available updates.

If you're using a Mac or Windows PC, reinstall the printer driver. Go to your system’s "Printers & Scanners" (or "Devices and Printers") and remove the printer. Then, re-add it using the manufacturer’s software or the "Add a Printer" wizard.

Lastly, try connecting the printer directly to your phone or tablet using the manufacturer’s app. If it works there, the issue is likely with your computer’s network or driver.

If all else fails, try a temporary workaround: connect the printer via USB to a computer, share it over the network, and print from other devices. This can get you through today while you figure out the wireless fix.

You’ve got this—take a deep breath, go through these steps one by one, and you’ll be printing wirelessly in no time. 💪🖨️

• The IP address is assigned (not 0.0.0.0 or 169.x.x.x).
• It’s not on a guest or isolated network.

2. RESTART THE PRINTER AND ROUTER (PROPERLY)

• Turn off the printer, unplug it for 30 seconds, then plug it back in.
• Do the same with your router (unplug for 30 seconds).
• Wait 1–2 minutes for both to fully reboot, then try adding the printer again.

3. USE THE PRINTER’S IP ADDRESS (MANUAL ADD)

Since the printer is on the network, you can add it manually:
• On the printer, go to Network Settings > Print Network Configuration Page.
• This will print a page with the printer’s IP address (e.g., 192.168.1.105).
• On your laptop:
  • Open Settings > Devices > Printers & Scanners > Add a printer or scanner.
  • Click "The printer that I want isn’t listed" > Add a printer using a TCP/IP address or hostname.
  • Enter the IP address from the printout.
  • Select the correct driver (if prompted—use the manufacturer’s website to download it).

4. CHECK FIREWALL & NETWORK DISCOVERY

• On your laptop:
  • Open Control Panel > Network and Sharing Center > Change advanced sharing settings.
  • Turn on Network Discovery and File and Printer Sharing for your network type (private).
• Also, check your Windows Defender Firewall or third-party antivirus—temporarily disable it to test if it’s blocking the printer.
